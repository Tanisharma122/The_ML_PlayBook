How an LLM reads: tokenization

A language model cannot read letters. It reads numbers. Tokenization is the step that turns your text into those numbers, and back again.

The colored tiles are tokens; the small numbers are their IDs. This page uses a small toy tokenizer so you can see the idea. Real models use the same method with a vocabulary of tens of thousands of tokens.

Characters: tiny vocabulary, long sequences. Words: short sequences, huge vocabulary, and new words break it. Subwords sit in between. Notice how text the toy vocabulary has never seen (like Hindi) falls back to single characters: the same thing makes non-English text costlier in real models.

Why subwords win

Word-level

Short sequences and easy to read. But the vocabulary is huge, and an unknown word like "ChatGPTify" has no entry at all.

Character-level

Tiny vocabulary and nothing is unknown. But sequences get very long and the model must learn spelling before it learns meaning.

Subword (used by LLMs)

Common words stay whole. Rare words split into reusable pieces: "unbelievable" becomes "un", "believ", "able".

The trade-off (conceptual sketch)

A bigger vocabulary shortens sequences but grows the embedding table. Real LLMs pick a middle value, commonly tens of thousands to a couple of hundred thousand tokens.

BPE step by step

Byte Pair Encoding builds its vocabulary by repeatedly merging the most frequent neighbouring pair. Press Merge and watch it learn from a tiny corpus (the counts are word frequencies).

The rule: 1) start with characters, 2) count adjacent pairs, 3) merge the most frequent, 4) repeat until the vocabulary reaches the size you want. Frequent chunks like "est" and "low" become single tokens.

Example IDs come from the GPT-2 tokenizer; other models use different vocabularies and give different IDs. After generation, the output IDs are decoded back to text.

The main algorithms

BPE

Merges the most frequent pair, repeatedly. Used in the GPT family (GPT-2 onward uses a byte-level version).

WordPiece

Like BPE, but picks merges that best improve the likelihood of the training data. Used by BERT; continuation pieces start with ##.

SentencePiece

Works on raw text and treats the space as a symbol (▁), so it is language-independent. Offers BPE or Unigram. Used by T5 and early LLaMA models.

Byte-level BPE

Starts from bytes, so any character in any language can be encoded and nothing is ever "unknown".

Special tokens are reserved entries with a job, such as start of text, end of text, padding, or chat role markers. Using the wrong ones with a model is a quiet way to get bad results.

Why it matters in practice

Cost and limits

APIs bill per token and context windows are counted in tokens. A common rule of thumb is about 4 characters, or roughly ¾ of an English word, per token.

Languages

Text in languages that were less common in the training data often needs more tokens per word, so it costs more and fills the context faster. Measure it with a real tokenizer.

Odd behaviour

The model sees chunks, not letters. That is why counting letters in a word, reversing strings or long exact arithmetic can go wrong.

Spaces and case

"hello", " hello" and "Hello" are different tokens with different IDs.

Always use the tokenizer that matches your model. Another model's vocabulary maps IDs to different pieces.

Self-check

Why not just use whole words?The vocabulary becomes enormous and any unseen word cannot be represented. Subwords build rare words from reusable pieces.
What does BPE merge at each step?The most frequent pair of adjacent tokens in the training text, until the vocabulary reaches the target size.
Why can LLMs struggle with letter counting?They receive tokens (chunks of characters), not individual letters, so spelling is not directly visible to them.
Can you swap tokenizers between models?No. The model was trained on one vocabulary; a different one maps IDs to different text.
Key takeawaysTokens are numbers for text pieces. Subwords balance vocabulary size and sequence length. Tokens set your cost and context limit. Always match tokenizer to model.
